    How this works

    Every few days the European Space Agency's Sentinel-2 satellites photograph the region at 10 m resolution. An automated pipeline downloads each new image, removes clouds and shadows, and checks every water pixel on the river:

    • Floating vegetation: water pixels that look strongly green to the satellite (NDVI above 0.3). On Pune's rivers this is almost always water hyacinth. Spots that stay green all year, before and after the monsoon flood, are trees, islands or farmed sandbars; they are left out of every number. Seasonal weeds on banks can still be counted.
    • Muddy water: water that reflects more red than green (NDTI above 0.05), a sign of sediment or sewage.

    These are satellite estimates, not lab measurements. Narrow stretches under 20 m wide and anything smaller than a pixel (plastic, garbage) cannot be seen. During the monsoon optical images are mostly cloudy, so only the radar estimate continues.
